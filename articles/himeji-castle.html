Himeji Castle

Japan's best-preserved original castle

Himeji Castle, in Hyogo Prefecture, is widely considered the finest surviving example of Japanese castle architecture. Unlike most of Japan's castles, which are concrete reconstructions built after wartime destruction, Himeji's main keep and much of its complex date back to the early 1600s and have survived earthquakes, fires, and World War II air raids largely intact.

The castle's brilliant white plaster exterior has earned it the nickname Shirasagijo, or "White Heron Castle," for its resemblance to a bird taking flight. Beyond the striking main keep, the grounds include a maze-like network of gates, corridors, and defensive walls designed to confuse and slow attackers — a layout that rewards a slow walk as much as a quick photo stop.

In 1993, Himeji Castle became one of Japan's first UNESCO World Heritage Sites, recognized alongside its surrounding gardens and defensive architecture as an outstanding example of Japanese castle construction. It remains one of the country's most visited landmarks, particularly during cherry blossom season when the castle grounds fill with blooming trees.
